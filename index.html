EARTH CLIMATE SYSTEM

Research focus

From climate states
to climate transitions.

My research combines coupled climate modelling, nonlinear dynamics, and paleoclimate to understand the emergence and stability of Earth's multiple equilibrium states and the transitions between them.

A central question is how the Earth's background state shapes its response to external forcing. I am particularly interested in the relationship between stability, feedbacks, climate sensitivity, and tipping across different climate states.

Research

Three perspectives on
Earth-system dynamics.

01

Climate multistability

How many different climates can Earth sustain?

I investigate multiple equilibrium climate states under different atmospheric CO₂ concentrations and Earth-system boundary conditions, and characterize their stability and transitions.

02

Tipping & climate networks

Can we detect an approaching climate tipping point?

I develop climate-network approaches to identify early warning signals and characterize unstable states associated with tipping in the climate system.

03

Earth-system modelling

How do slow Earth-system components shape climate?

I lead the development of BIG-MITgcm, a coupled ocean–atmosphere– ice-sheet–vegetation–hydrology framework for exploring climate states and long-timescale feedbacks.

01

Earth-system modelling

BIG-MITgcm

A framework for exploring Earth's multiple climate states.

BIG-MITgcm couples ocean, atmosphere, ice sheets, vegetation and hydrology to investigate equilibrium climate states and long-timescale Earth-system feedbacks. The model provides an intermediate-complexity framework for exploring how slow components of the Earth system influence climate trajectories.

Climate tipping

The AMOC edge state

A spatial signature of the unstable state between contrasting circulation regimes.

Using climate networks, I investigated the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation edge state — the state located on the basin boundary separating the ON and OFF regimes of the AMOC.

About me

Physics, nonlinear dynamics & Earth-system science.

I am a PhD researcher and graduate teaching assistant in the Physics Section at the University of Geneva, Switzerland.

My academic background is in physics, with a specialization in applied physics. My doctoral research focuses on climate multistability and transitions, from Earth's past to its potential future trajectories.
