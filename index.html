EARTH CLIMATE SYSTEM

Research focus

From climate states
to climate transitions.

My research combines coupled climate modelling, nonlinear dynamics, and paleoclimate to understand the emergence, stability, and transitions between Earth's multiple equilibrium climate states.

A central question of my work is how the Earth's background state shapes its response to external forcing — and whether dynamical properties such as stability can help us understand climate sensitivity and approaching tipping points.

Research

Three perspectives on
Earth-system dynamics.

01

Climate multistability

How many different climates can Earth sustain?

I investigate the existence and stability of multiple equilibrium climate states under different atmospheric CO₂ concentrations and Earth-system boundary conditions.

Explore the research →
02

Tipping & climate networks

Can we detect an approaching climate tipping point?

I develop network-based methods to identify early warning signals and characterize unstable states associated with tipping in the climate system.

Explore the research →
03

Earth-system modelling

How do the slow components of Earth shape its climate?

I lead the development of BIG-MITgcm, a coupled ocean–atmosphere–ice-sheet–vegetation–hydrology framework for exploring equilibrium climate states and long-timescale feedbacks.

The AMOC edge state

A spatial signature of the unstable state separating contrasting ocean circulation regimes.

Using climate networks, I investigated the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation edge state — the state located on the basin boundary separating the ON and OFF regimes of the AMOC.
